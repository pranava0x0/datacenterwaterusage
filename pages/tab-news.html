UC Riverside 'Making AI Less Thirsty' — First Major Quantification of AI Water Footprint
University of California, Riverside (Shaolei Ren et al.) · 2023-06
The landmark study estimated 0.5 L per 10–50 ChatGPT queries (direct cooling only) and up to 500 mL per query when including thermoelectric cooling for electricity generation. It catalyzed policy and media attention on data center water use and established WUE (Water Usage Effectiveness) as a public-facing metric. The viral 'one 500mL bottle per prompt' figure in later coverage was a 50–250× overstatement of this research.

NC State University College of Natural Resources · 2026-09-03
A synthesis featuring hydrologist Matthias Sprenger, drawing on Ceres and Global Energy Monitor, puts US data centers' direct cooling consumption at about 17 billion gallons in 2023, against an estimated 3.4 trillion gallons of withdrawals for the electricity serving data centers in just seven states in 2024 — with more than 189 GW of gas generation planned for data centers by mid-2026. EPA's Water Reuse Action Plan 2.0 Names Data-Center Cooling a Priority
US Environmental Protection Agency · 2026-04-16
EPA launched WRAP 2.0 as a collaborative, non-regulatory framework under existing Clean Water Act and Safe Drinking Water Act authority. One of three headline initiatives, 'Water for the U.S. Technology Revolution', commits EPA to compile best practices so states can more readily permit reclaimed water for data-center cooling and to identify regulatory hurdles to that reuse. It is the closest thing to a national water strategy that addresses data centers, and it is voluntary.
