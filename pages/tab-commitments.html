National strategy

No U.S. national water strategy addresses data centers. The federal water assessment does not name them as a demand; EPA's reuse plan names data-center cooling but is voluntary; the executive orders speed permitting up rather than set a water standard; and the disclosure bills are pending. The countries that do have a national instrument — the EU and Singapore — show what one looks like.
United States In force Voluntary
Water Reuse Action Plan 2.0 (WRAP 2.0) · 2026-04-16

A collaborative, explicitly non-regulatory framework under existing Clean Water Act and Safe Drinking Water Act authority. Data-center cooling is one of the named priority reuse sectors, and Action 3.10 is the closest thing the federal government has to a data-center water commitment: helping states permit reclaimed water for cooling.

  • Reclaimed or non-potable water Compile and share best practices for industrial cooling so states can more readily permit recycled or reclaimed water for data-center cooling (Action 3.10).
  • Study or assessment Identify, and propose fixes to, the regulatory hurdles that block reuse for data-center cooling.
Related: Reclaimed / Recycled Water Sourcing
Sources: EPA — Water Reuse Action Plan 2.0 · EPA news release — EPA launches Water Reuse Action Plan 2.0
United States In force Study only
National Water Availability Assessment (water years 2010-2020) · 2025-01-16

The federal government's national picture of water supply and demand, which finds about 30 million people live in areas of high water limitation. Data centers do not appear in it as a water-use category or a demand driver — the assessment that would anchor a national strategy does not see them.

  • Study or assessment National supply-and-demand assessment for water years 2010-2020; demand drivers discussed are agriculture, energy production, ecosystems and population growth, not data centers.
Sources: USGS — Integrated Water Availability Assessments · USGS news release — a comprehensive look at water resources in the United States

Other countries — the benchmark

European Union In force Reporting duty
Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364 — common Union rating scheme for data centres · 2024-03-14

Under the recast Energy Efficiency Directive, every data centre with 500 kW or more of installed IT load must measure and report its total water input and its potable water input, and the Commission computes water usage effectiveness as one of four official sustainability indicators. The EU already has, in binding law, the facility-level water reporting US bills are still proposing — though individual figures stay confidential and only aggregates are published.

  • Disclosure & reporting Mandatory reporting of total water input and potable water input (Annex II) for data centres of 500 kW or more; aggregate WUE published in the European database on data centres.
Sources: EUR-Lex — Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364
European Union In force Strategy
European Water Resilience Strategy (COM(2025) 280) · 2025-06-04

A national-scale water strategy that names data centres explicitly, among the strategic sectors that 'consume large volumes of often ultra-pure water'. It commits the Commission to put water usage in the data-centre sustainability rating and to propose minimum water-consumption performance standards for data centres — a move from reporting to an efficiency floor, with no numeric target yet.

  • Disclosure & reporting Include water usage among the parameters of the common Union scheme rating data-centre sustainability.
  • Efficiency target Propose minimum water-consumption performance standards for data centres (no number set yet).
Sources: EUR-Lex — European Water Resilience Strategy, COM(2025) 280 final
Singapore In force Strategy
Green Data Centre Roadmap · 2024-05-30

Sets a national water-use-effectiveness goal: bring data centres from a 2021 median of 2.2 m³/MWh to 2.0 m³/MWh or lower within ten years, mainly by optimizing cooling towers, which are up to 97% of a data centre's water use. Separately and already binding, any site using 60,000 m³ a year or more must meter privately and file a Water Efficiency Plan with PUB.

  • Efficiency target National WUE target of 2.0 m³/MWh or lower within ten years (from a 2.2 m³/MWh median in 2021).
  • Disclosure & reporting Sites using 60,000 m³ a year or more: private water meters and a Water Efficiency Plan filed with PUB (binding).
Sources: IMDA — Green Data Centre Roadmap · IMDA factsheet — Green DC Roadmap

How the columns are read

Each column is the set of principle tags that express it:

  • Disclose water use — Disclosure, Transparency, NDA prohibition
  • Review before building — Preemptive review, Permit oversight
  • Pay its own way — Cost allocation
  • Use less water — Conservation, Closed-loop cooling
  • Condition incentives — Anti-corporate-welfare
  • Pause development — Moratorium
  • Issue guidance — Best-practice guidance

Local agreements

The most specific water commitments in this record are in deals between a locality and a developer: gallon-a-day caps, cooling-technology bans, restoration money. They bind only the one project, which is exactly why they can be precise.

Doña Ana County, NM In force Binding
Industrial revenue bond and PILOT agreement (Ordinance 367-2025) · 2025-09-15

The campus serving the Oracle and OpenAI Stargate buildout carries the largest water-infrastructure commitment in this record: $50 million for public water and wastewater infrastructure in the Sunland Park and Santa Teresa area, $10 million of it as metrics-driven water-quality grants to Sunland Park, plus $250,000 to evaluate a desalination plant.

  • Developer-funded water infrastructure $50 million for public water and wastewater infrastructure in Doña Ana County.
  • Developer-funded water infrastructure $10 million of it routed to Sunland Park as a metrics-driven water-quality grant program.
  • Study or assessment $250,000 to evaluate a desalination plant as a long-term drinking-water source.
Related: minimize water use by prioritizing closed-loop or low-water cooling systems

The $50 million and $10 million figures come from the developer's release; the county's own page lists the desalination evaluation and generic water and wastewater improvements.

Sources: STACK Infrastructure — $56.9 million in water and community commitments for Doña Ana County · Doña Ana County — economic development projects
Lancaster, PA In force Binding
Community benefits agreement · 2025-11

One of the first data-center community benefits agreements with a numeric water term: the agreement restricts the facility's water use to 20,000 gallons a day.

  • Water-use cap Water use restricted to 20,000 gallons a day.

The city's posted draft is a compressed PDF with no extractable text; the cap is as reported by Brookings and local coverage.

Mount Pleasant, WI In force Binding
Development agreement (former Foxconn land) · 2023-11-27

Microsoft's agreement to build on the former Foxconn site pairs the data centers with watershed restoration: $4.2 million to restore 1.5 miles of Lamparek Creek and about 100 acres of surrounding prairie and forest, and $100,000 for water restoration across Racine County.

  • Restoration & replenishment $4.2 million to restore 1.5 miles of Lamparek Creek (about 100 acres of prairie, forest and degraded stream buffer).
  • Restoration & replenishment $100,000 for water-restoration work across Racine County with the Root-Pike Watershed Initiative Network.

Pima County, AZ In force Binding
Memorandum of Agreement (Project Blue), approved 3-2 · 2025-12-16

After Tucson rejected Project Blue, the county's agreement with the developer commits the campus to closed-loop air cooling, limits water use to domestic needs such as restrooms and kitchens, and requires the Arizona Department of Water Resources to approve its water sources.

  • Closed-loop or dry cooling A closed-loop air-cooling system keeps the facility from overheating.
  • Water-use cap Water consumption limited to domestic necessities such as bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Approval condition Water sources must be approved by the Arizona Department of Water Resources.
Related: Project Blue — Tucson / Pima County, AZ

The agreement itself is posted as a scanned PDF with no extractable text; terms are as reported by Arizona Luminaria.

Sources: Arizona Luminaria — Pima County supervisors approve agreement outlining Beale's commitments on Project Blue
Saline Township, MI In force Binding
Consent judgment, Washtenaw County Circuit Court · 2025-10

The settlement of the rezoning lawsuit bars high-water-use evaporative cooling, limits water use to domestic, humidity, landscaping, fire-protection and maintenance needs (about 20,000 gallons a day anticipated), and preserves 47.5 acres of wetlands under a conservation easement. The facility is not water-free: its air-cooled system circulates a water-refrigerant mix filled from an on-site well.

  • Closed-loop or dry cooling No design containing high-water-use evaporative cooling (Section 3(g)).
  • Water-use cap Water use limited to restrooms and domestic uses, humidity, landscaping, fire protection and maintenance; about 20,000 gallons a day anticipated.
  • Restoration & replenishment 47.5 acres of wetlands protected by a conservation easement.
Related: minimize water use by prioritizing closed-loop or low-water cooling systems

County Executive Jessica Fitzwater rejected the proposed agreement amid public outcry. It had offered to shrink planned development by about a fifth and cut potable water use by 80% once a reclaimed-water system came online — commitments that never took effect.

  • Reclaimed or non-potable water Cut potable water use 80% once a reclaimed-water system came online (offered, not adopted).
