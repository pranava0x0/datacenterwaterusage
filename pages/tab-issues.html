Google — The Dalles, ORDC site
The Dalles, Wasco County, OregonCWASDWASLSecrecy & FOIA fightsMunicipal supply strainDisclosure gapResolved — data disclosed; the case is now the template other communities cite when operators claim water use is confidential.
Water issue

Google's water use nearly tripled in five years to over a quarter of everything the city supplies (later reporting puts it near 40%). When The Oregonian requested the numbers in 2021, the city — with Google paying its legal fees — sued the newspaper to keep them secret as a 'trade secret.'

Pushback & response

The district attorney sided with the newspaper; the city settled in late 2022 and released ten years of data, and Google said it would stop claiming site-level water figures as trade secrets nationwide — the landmark transparency precedent for the industry.

A single customer taking a quarter-plus of a small city's supply concentrates public-water-system capacity and compliance risk on everyone else's taps — the SDWA regime that receivership-era enforcement polices.

Cooling water Google evaporates or returns goes through the municipal system; blowdown returned to the sewer sits under the POTW's industrial-user pretreatment authority.
Historical cases: PortOfMorrow DEQ OR (2024)
SL — Water claims as a deceptive trade practice
The precedent that made every later water claim checkable. Litigation here forced release of a decade of figures and prompted Google to stop asserting site-level water use as a trade secret nationwide — the disclosure that consumer-protection theories now build on.

Water issue

Colossus draws roughly 3 MGD of cooling water while the Memphis Sand aquifer — the region's sole drinking-water source — is already being pumped faster than it recharges. xAI promised an $80M recycling plant treating up to ~13 MGD of municipal wastewater to offset withdrawals, then put it on indefinite pause in April 2026 to prioritize Colossus 2.

Which legal readings could apply
CWA — NPDES permit for direct discharge
The promised recycling plant would hold its own NPDES permit — a clean, trackable point source whose DMRs become public the day it operates.
Historical cases: PortOfMorrow DEQ OR (2024)
CWA — Pretreatment — industrial users of sewers

SDWA — Source-water and wellhead protection

Historical cases: Google Berkeley SC Middendorf aquifer (2019)
EQAP — Equitable apportionment reaches groundwater
The Memphis Sand is the same aquifer the Supreme Court held apportionable in 2021, so the question of who may complain about heavy pumping here is already answered: a neighbouring state, suing for an equitable apportionment. The obstacle is evidentiary rather than legal — an apportioning court needs clear and convincing proof of injury, which means aggregate withdrawal data of the kind utility reporting laws produce.
Historical cases: Mississippi v Tennessee (2021)

No federal reservation overlies or adjoins the Memphis Sand in this reach, so no senior reserved right competes for the water. Reserved rights are decisive in the arid Southwest and simply absent here.
Historical cases: Winters v United States (1908)

Meta — Newton County / Mansfield, GADC site
Social Circle / Mansfield area, Newton County, GeorgiaCWASDWAPTDCLAquifer depletionActive civil litigation filed September 2026; allegations contested and not adjudicated. No formal environmental enforcement identified.
Water issue

After Meta broke ground on its $750M campus, nearby well-dependent households — the closest about 1,000 feet away — reported taps running dry, sediment, and pressure loss. The facility uses ~500,000 gal/day (~10% of county water use), and Mansfield's mayor cited 33% water-rate increases over two years. Meta's commissioned study called its impact 'unlikely.'

Pushback & response

The July 2025 New York Times investigation made this the national reference case for data-center groundwater impacts; EPA pledged investigation of adjoining Morgan County complaints. In September 2026, several Newton County homeowners filed a civil suit against Meta and the four-county development authority alleging construction-related aquifer disturbance, turbidity, sediment, clogged lines, and appliance damage. These are allegations, not adjudicated findings. Meta denies causation, says commissioned analysis found groundwater flowing away from plaintiffs' properties, and says the campus uses local-utility water.

Which legal readings could apply
SDWA — Emergency powers — imminent endangerment of drinking water
If construction dewatering or site releases are shown to have contaminated or dried domestic wells, EPA's emergency powers reach it on a 'contributed to' standard without waiting for a permit violation.
Historical cases: Trinity American v EPA SDWA (1431-1998) · DuPont WashingtonWorks PFOA (2005-2009)
SDWA — Source-water and wellhead protection
Wellhead-protection delineations around the affected domestic and municipal wells are the evidentiary frame for the county's zoning and permitting response.
Historical cases: Google Berkeley SC Middendorf aquifer (2019)
CWA — Construction stormwater (CGP)
The reported sediment in neighbors' water during mass grading is the classic construction-general-permit fact pattern.
Historical cases: Google Stillwater OK stormwater (2025) · Microsoft Boydton VA (2023)
CL — Capture shields depletion, not negligent harm
Georgia's reasonable-use approach to groundwater gives failed-well neighbours a direct common-law theory that a landowner in a strict rule-of-capture state would lack. The households nearest the campus are the strongest available plaintiffs precisely because their injury is concrete and individual.
Historical cases: Friendswood v Smith Southwest (1978) · MCWC v Nestle Waters (2007)
PTD — The trust follows groundwater into surface flow
Where campus drawdown measurably reduces flow in a connected navigable stream, the trust duty attaches to the well permit itself, and the duty sits with the permitting authority rather than the operator. Requires hydrologic evidence of the groundwater-to-surface connection.
Historical cases: ELF v SWRCB (2018)

Historical cases: ProjectBlue Tucson AMES (2026)

Historical cases: US v Klamath Drainage District (2025)
